## Biography

I am Junjie Xia, a Ph.D. candidate in Chemistry at the University of Chicago. My research focuses on building single-cell and spatial multi-omic technologies that measure protein abundance, protein interactions, glycosylation states, and transcriptional programs with direct relevance to immunology and translational biomarker discovery.

My training sits at the intersection of assay development and computational analysis. On the experimental side, I work on antibody and protein conjugation, next-generation sequencing workflows, proximity-labeling strategies, flow cytometry, and microscopy. On the computational side, I build analysis pipelines in Python and R for single-cell and spatial datasets, with an emphasis on extracting interpretable biological signals from high-dimensional measurements.

Before starting my doctoral work, I received my B.S. in Polymer Materials and Engineering from Zhejiang University. I also spent time in biotech-focused venture capital, where I evaluated therapeutic platforms and development strategies. That experience sharpened how I think about translational biology, drug development, and the path from scientific insight to useful technology.

## Research Focus

My current work centers on platform development for probing protein biology beyond transcript abundance alone. I am particularly interested in technologies that resolve extracellular proteins, protein complexes, glycoforms, and tissue-contextual interactions at single-cell or spatial resolution.

Two platforms that define this work are:

- **Glycan Proximity Sequencing (GPS)**, which integrates measurement of proteins, glycans, and mRNA in single cells.
- **Spatial Proximity Sequencing (SProx)**, which profiles protein abundance and protein interactions directly within intact tissue.

Across these systems, my goal is to create molecular readouts that improve both mechanistic understanding and translational decision-making.

## Translational Interests

I am interested in how advanced measurement technologies can improve target discovery, biomarker strategy, translational profiling, and therapeutic development. More broadly, I am motivated by problems where rigorous molecular profiling can help connect basic biology to clinically meaningful decisions.
